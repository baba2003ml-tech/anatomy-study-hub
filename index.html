Skeletal System10 topics
Overview & Composition
Long bone anatomy — compact & spongy bone, marrow, periosteum
Full skeleton — axial (blue) & appendicular (beige) bones labeled
  • The skeletal system is composed of bones, joints, cartilage, ligaments, and tendons
  • The body has 206 bones
  • Bone tissue is living tissue containing inorganic substances like calcium and phosphorus
  • Bones are hard, rigid structures covered by a membrane called a periosteum
  • The skeleton gives us our basic shape
Functions of the Skeletal System

1. Support

  • The main job of the skeleton is to provide support for the body
  • Without a skeleton, the body would collapse into a heap
  • The skeleton is strong but light

2. Protection

  • Bones protect important organs (brain by cranium, heart by thoracic cage)

3. Movement

  • Bones provide the structure for muscles to attach so our bodies can move
  • Tendons are tough inelastic bands that attach muscle to bone

4. Stores Important Minerals

  • Calcium: Helps muscles, nerves, and cells work normally; 90% stored in bones and teeth; body cannot make calcium
  • Phosphorus: Works with calcium to build bones; requires vitamin D for proper absorption

5. Blood Cell Production & Storage

  • In some bones is jelly-like bone marrow, where new blood cells are produced
  • Red marrow: Produces red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets
  • Yellow marrow: Stored as fat; produces some white blood cells
Types of Bone Tissue
Bone cross-section — compact & spongy bone, red & yellow marrow

Compact Bone

  • Hard and dense
  • Composes the shaft of long bones and the outer layer of bones

Spongy Bone

  • Light, with more spaces
  • Found at the end of long bones and center of other bones
Bone Shapes & Sizes

Long Bones

  • Bear the weight of the body (especially femur and tibia)
  • Longer than they are wide
  • Regions: Diaphysis (shaft), Epiphysis (ends), Epiphyseal disc (growth plate), Medullary cavity, Periosteum, Articular cartilage

Short Bones

  • Shaped like cubes; found in wrists (carpals) and ankles (tarsals)

Flat Bones

  • Thin, flat, and curved (ribs, sternum, cranium, shoulder)
  • Functions: protection, muscle attachment, blood cell production

Irregular Bones

  • Differently shaped (hip bones, vertebrae, some skull bones)
Bone Growth (Ossification)

Growing Taller

  • Occurs at the epiphyseal disc (growth plate)
  • Osteoblasts invade cartilage in the disc, mature into osteocytes
  • Affected by hormones: growth hormone, estrogen, testosterone
  • Estrogen fuses the growth plate earlier than testosterone

Epiphyseal Plate Injury

  • A break in the growth plate area can lead to decreased bone growth and a shortened or crooked limb

Stimulating Bone Growth

  • Weight bearing and exercise keeps calcium in bones and increases bone mass

Bone Surface Markings

  • Bumpy, irregular surface with ridges, projections, depressions, and grooves
  • Serve as attachment points for muscles, tendons, and ligaments
Axial Skeleton: Skull
Skull — Cranium (8 bones) + Face (14 bones) = 22 total

Cranium (8 bones)

  • Protects the brain: Frontal (1), Parietal (2), Temporal (2), Occipital (1), Sphenoid (1), Ethmoid (1)

Facial Bones (14)

  • Mandible (1), Maxilla (2), Palatine (2), Zygomatic (2), Lacrimal (2), Nasal (2), Vomer (1)

Sinuses

  • Air-filled cavities that lighten the skull and modulate voice: Frontal, Ethmoidal, Sphenoidal, Maxillary

Fontanels

  • Soft spots on an infant's skull, not yet converted to bone
  • Allow for cranial growth; bulging = increased pressure; sunken = dehydration

Hyoid Bone

  • Below mandible; U-shaped; floats in front of cervical vertebrae; anchors tongue; swallowing

Ear Ossicles (3 per ear)

  • Malleus, Incus, Stapes
Vertebral Column
Vertebral column — cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacrum & coccyx
  • Supports head and thorax; encases spinal cord; provides flexibility

Cervical (7) — Neck

  • Atlas (C1): Articulates with occipital bone; head nods YES
  • Axis (C2): Pivoting point; head says NO

Thoracic (12)

  • Each articulates with a pair of ribs (T1–T12)

Lumbar (5)

  • Largest and heaviest; most common site for slipped disc (L1–L5)

Sacrum & Coccyx

  • Sacrum: 5 fused bones. Coccyx: 3–5 fused bones
Thoracic Cage
Thoracic cage — sternum, ribs & xiphoid process
  • Sternum (breastbone)
  • 12 pairs of ribs: 7 true, 5 false (including 2 floating)
  • Each rib connects to a thoracic vertebra
  • Xiphoid process: Teardrop-shaped bone below sternum; must locate during CPR
Appendicular Skeleton
Upper limbs — humerus, radius, ulna, hand bones
Lower limbs — femur, tibia, fibula, foot bones

Upper Limbs

  • Humerus: Arm bone; fits into scapula cavity; allows arm rotation
  • Radius: Lateral (thumb) side of forearm
  • Ulna: Longer bone, medial side; olecranon process = bony elbow
  • Hand: 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges

Pelvic Girdle

  • Weight bearing, lower limb attachment, pelvic organ protection

Lower Limbs

  • Femur: Longest & strongest bone; forms hip joint and knee
  • Acetabulum: Receives head of femur; allows thigh rotation
  • Patella: Kneecap; protects front of knee
  • Tibia: Shin bone; medial malleolus at distal end
  • Fibula: Thin, beside tibia; not part of knee; lateral malleolus

Foot

  • Ankle: 7 tarsals (talus & calcaneus bear most weight)
  • Instep: 5 metatarsals
  • Toes: 14 phalanges
Joints7 topics
Joints Overview
Skull sutures — immovable joints holding cranium together
  • Functions: Hold bones together; allow movement
  • Rheumatology: Branch of science that studies joint diseases
Types of Joints

Immovable

  • Permit no movement (e.g. skull sutures)

Slightly Movable

Slightly movable joints — vertebrae & symphysis pubis
  • Examples: symphysis pubis, vertebral column joints

Freely Movable (Synovial)

  • Allow the greatest range of motion
Synovial Joint Composition
Synovial joint — capsule, cartilage, synovial fluid & membrane
Synovial joint cross-section — bursa & meniscus
  • Articular cartilage: Smooth surface within the joint
  • Meniscus: Only in the knee; C-shaped; shock absorber
  • Cartilage: Made of collagen & elastin; protects, smooths movement, absorbs shock
  • Joint capsule: Fibrous sleeve enclosing the joint
  • Synovial membrane: Lines capsule; secretes synovial fluid
  • Synovial fluid: Lubricates bones; consistency of egg white
  • Bursa: Fluid-filled sac reducing friction; protects tendons
  • Supporting ligaments: Join bones, stabilize joint; can tear or stretch
Types of Freely Movable Joints
  • Hinge: Elbows, knees, fingers, jaw
  • Ball-and-socket: Shoulders, hips
  • Pivot: Atlas-axis joint
  • Saddle: Thumb
  • Gliding: Wrist, spine, ankles
Joint Names & Specific Joints
  • Tibiofemoral (knee): Hinge; extra cartilage padding; meniscus easily torn
  • Humeroulnar (elbow): Hinge joint
  • Metacarpophalangeal (wrist): Metacarpal & phalange
  • Glenohumeral (shoulder): Ball-and-socket; great ROM; easily dislocated
  • Coxal (hip): Ball-and-socket; femur & acetabulum; fractures common
Types of Joint Movement
  • Flexion-Extension
  • Inversion-Eversion
  • Supination-Pronation
  • Abduction-Adduction
  • Dorsiflexion-Plantar flexion
  • Hyperextension
  • Circumduction
Tendons & Ligaments
Tendons (muscle→bone) vs. Ligaments (bone→bone)
Knee ligaments — ACL, PCL, MCL & LCL

Tendons

  • Cordlike fibrous connective tissue
  • Attach muscle to bone
  • Provide traction for movement

Ligaments

  • Fibrous connective tissue
  • Attach bone to bone
  • Stretchy (prevents tearing when joints bend)
  • Prevent dislocations
Muscular System9 topics
Muscle Facts
Muscle nicknames — common names you already know
  • More than 600 muscles in the body
  • Made up of special muscle fibers
  • Largest: gluteus maximus
  • Smallest: stapedius (inner ear)
  • 40–50% of total body weight
  • Work in pairs: one shortens, the other lengthens
  • Busiest muscles control eye movements
  • Hardest working muscle: the heart
  • 85% of body heat from muscle contraction
  • Shivering = involuntary contraction to increase body temperature
Types of Muscles
3 muscle types — skeletal, smooth & cardiac

Skeletal Muscle

  • Striated and voluntary; causes movement of the skeleton

Smooth Muscle (Visceral)

  • Nonstriated and involuntary; enables organs to function

Cardiac Muscle

  • Found only in the heart; striated and involuntary; cells fit tightly to conduct electrical signals
Skeletal Muscle Functions & Structure
Muscle structure — fascicles, fibers & myofibrils

Functions

  • Produce movement, maintain posture, stabilize joints, produce heat, limited regeneration

Structure

  • Muscle fibers → fascicle → muscle
  • Fascia: Connective tissue surrounding organs, muscles, etc.
  • Layers: Epimysium (outer), Perimysium (middle), Endomysium (inner)
  • Tendons: Attach muscle to bone for movement
Muscle Parts & Naming

Parts

  • Origin: Where it starts (no movement)
  • Body: Center / belly of the muscle
  • Insertion: Where it ends (movement occurs)

Naming Conventions (7 ways)

  • Size, Shape, Direction of fibers, Location, Number of origins, Origin & insertion, Muscle action
Muscle Terminology
  • Tonus: Partial contraction present in all healthy muscles at rest
  • Hypertrophy: Growth from overuse
  • Atrophy: Wasting from disuse, poor innervation, or aging
  • Contracture: Abnormal fibrous formation; freezes in flexed position
Muscles: Head & Neck
Anterior (front) muscles — all major muscles labeled

Facial Muscles

  • Frontalis: Raises eyebrows, wrinkles forehead
  • Orbicularis oculi: Closes eye; winking, blinking
  • Levator palpebrae superioris: Lifts eyelid
  • Orbicularis oris: Closes mouth, forms words
  • Buccinator: Flattens cheek; positions food
  • Zygomaticus: Smiling muscle
  • Platysma: Pouting, opening mouth wide

Chewing (Mastication)

  • Masseter & Temporalis: Close the jaw

Neck

  • Sternocleidomastoid: Flexion & rotation of head
  • Scalene muscles (3): Synergists with sternocleidomastoid
  • Trapezius: Extension & hyperextension of head
Muscles: Trunk
Trunk muscles — abdominal wall & obliques
Back muscles — erector spinae & latissimus dorsi

Breathing

  • Intercostal muscles (internal & external)
  • Diaphragm: Primarily responsible for breathing

Abdominal Wall

  • Internal oblique, External oblique, Transversus abdominis, Rectus abdominis

Back

  • Erector spinae: Cranium to sacrum; maintains erect posture
Muscles: Shoulder, Arm & Forearm

Shoulder & Arm

  • Trapezius: Shrugs shoulders
  • Serratus anterior: Lowers shoulders, pushing
  • Pectoralis major: Moves arm across chest
  • Latissimus dorsi: “Swimmer’s muscle”
  • Deltoid: Abducts arm; injection site
  • Teres major: Extends arm at shoulder
  • Rotator cuff: Stabilizes shoulder; injury-prone

Forearm

  • Biceps brachii: Flexes forearm
  • Triceps brachii: Extends forearm; push-ups
  • Brachialis & Brachioradialis: Flex forearm
  • Supinators & Pronators: Rotate forearm
Muscles: Leg & Foot
Posterior (back) muscles — all major muscles labeled
Leg muscles — quads, hamstrings, calves & more

Anterior (Front)

  • Quadriceps femoris (4): Rectus femoris, Vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius — extend leg at knee
  • Sartorius: Cross-legged position
  • Tibialis anterior: Dorsiflexion & inversion
  • Peroneus longus: Everts foot, supports arch

Posterior (Back)

  • Hamstrings (3): Biceps femoris, Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus — leg flexion, thigh extension
  • Gastrocnemius & Soleus: Form the calf; plantar flexion
  • Achilles (calcaneal) tendon: Connects calf to heel
Pre & Post Op Care18 topics
Pre-Operative Care Overview

Care and procedures done before a person has surgery.

  • Surgery can be emergency or elective (scheduled)
  • This is a general guide for ALL surgical procedures
  • Always be aware of specific care for specific surgeries
Pre-Op Checklist (Day of Surgery)

Everything that needs to happen before the patient goes to the OR.

Skin Prep & Hygiene
  • Bath/shower the night before AND morning of surgery with chlorhexidine scrub (antibacterial soap)
  • Reduces risk of infection at surgical site
  • No deodorant, perfume, or lotion — leaves residue hard to wash off
  • All nail polish removed — so pulse ox can read oxygen saturation
ID, Allergies & Jewelry
  • Patient must wear bracelet with correct identification
  • Allergy bracelet must be on
  • Remove all jewelry, piercings, and contact lenses
Consent & Bloodwork
  • No surgery without a signed consent
  • LPN/Nurse ensures consent is in the chart
  • Doctor’s responsibility to ensure it is signed
  • Recent bloodwork results must be in chart
NPO, Elimination & Clothing
  • NPO (nothing by mouth) — minimizes aspiration risk during anesthesia
  • May take essential meds with doctor’s permission
  • Can rinse mouth or brush teeth without swallowing
  • Patient must void before going to OR — prevents retention/incontinence during surgery
  • Bowel evacuation recommended morning of surgery
  • Must wear hospital gown, remove underwear
Prosthesis & Shave Prep
  • Dentures must be removed — ensure proper identification and store safely
  • Hearing aids and glasses may go with patient if impairment is profound
  • Limb prosthesis removed
  • Shave prep: depends on hospital policy — some surgeons prefer to do it in OR
Psychological Preparation
  • Surgery is always a source of stress
  • Advise patient when surgery will take place and how the scenario will play out
  • Discuss post-op meds and pain control
  • Discuss need for early mobilization
  • Teach deep breathing, coughing exercises, and how to use incentive spirometer
  • No surprises — make sure they know what’s going to happen
  • A well-informed patient is more relaxed
  • Keep the family informed — they can be more anxious than the patient
Room Prep & Waiting for Patient
  • Ensure room is prepared — clean, tidy, bed flat
  • Have all required equipment in room
  • Reassure the family who may be waiting
  • Inform family when patient is in recovery room
  • Prepare family for what to expect when patient returns to floor
Post-Op Care Goals

General post-operative care goals:

  • Ensure safety and comfort of the patient
  • Prevention of post-operative complications
  • Return patient to highest level of autonomy ASAP
7 P’s — Neurovascular Signs

Check these signs post-op:

  1. Pain
  2. Pallor
  3. Paresthesia (numbness)
  4. Paralysis
  5. Polar Temp (Poikilothermia)
  6. Puffiness (edema)
  7. Pulselessness
Incentive Spirometry & Early Mobilization
  • Incentive spirometry every hour (q1h)
  • Early mobilization is key to quicker recovery
  • Up in chair ASAP — day 0 or day 1
  • Even if there are tubes everywhere
  • Ensure adequate pain medication before ambulating
Post-Op Elimination & IV
  • Urinary retention is common post-op
  • Urine output must be monitored
  • Bladder scanner can be used
  • May need in-and-out catheterization if no urine output by prescribed time
  • Once patient is drinking well, IV is usually discontinued
Pain Management & PCA
  • Evaluate pain frequently
  • Patient won’t cooperate with mobilizing if they’re in pain
  • Use pain scale and PQRSTU assessment
  • PCA (Patient Controlled Analgesia) may be prescribed
  • Opioid side effects: constipation, weakness, dizziness, falls, respiratory depression
Wound Care & Drains
  • Monitor wound frequently for signs of bleeding
  • Demarcate (mark) any drainage on the dressing
  • Reinforce dressing if necessary — do not remove
  • Advise MD, document, and report
  • Empty drains every shift and PRN
  • Surgeon usually removes the initial dressing
  • Follow TNP for wound management
Opioids & Narcan
  • An opiate is a narcotic analgesic — natural or synthetic opium
  • Most important side effect to watch for: respiratory depression
  • Narcan (naloxone) reverses opioid effects
  • Narcotic naive = patient not used to opioids — higher risk of side effects
  • Only available by prescription
Pain Assessment (PQRSTU)

Pain effects: fatigue, decreased coping, potentially debilitating, slower recovery, decreased mobility.

PQRSTU:

  • P — Provocation / Palliation (what makes it worse/better)
  • Q — Quality (sharp, dull, burning)
  • R — Region / Radiation (where, does it spread)
  • S — Severity (0-10 scale)
  • T — Timing (when did it start, constant or intermittent)
  • U — Understanding (patient’s perception of pain)

Objective signs: what you can observe (facial grimacing, guarding, vital sign changes).

Subjective symptoms: what the patient tells you.

Non-Pharmacologic Pain Interventions
  • Positioning and repositioning
  • Ice/heat therapy
  • Distraction (music, TV, conversation)
  • Relaxation techniques and deep breathing
  • Massage
  • Guided imagery
Anatomie en Français8 topics
Le Système Squelettique

Le corps humain possède 206 os.

  • Les os longs (les os longs) — fémur, humérus, tibia
  • Les os courts (les os courts) — carpiens, tarsiens
  • Les os plats (les os plats) — crâne, côtes, sternum
  • Les os irréguliers (les os irréguliers) — vertèbres, os de la hanche

Termes clés :

  • Le périoste (periosteum) — membrane qui recouvre l'os
  • La moelle osseuse rouge (red bone marrow) — produit les cellules sanguines
  • La moelle osseuse jaune (yellow bone marrow) — stocke la graisse
  • L'os compact (compact bone) — dur et dense, forme la diaphyse
  • L'os spongieux (spongy bone) — léger, aux extrémités des os longs
Les Os du Crâne et de la Face
  • Le crâne (cranium) — 8 os : frontal, pariétaux (2), temporaux (2), occipital, sphénoïde, ethmoïde
  • Les os de la face (facial bones) — 14 os : mandibule, maxillaires, zygomatiques, os nasaux, etc.
  • Les fontanelles (fontanels) — espaces mous sur le crâne du nourrisson
  • Les sinus (sinuses) — cavités remplies d'air qui allègent le crâne
  • L'atlas (C1) — permet de hocher la tête (oui)
  • L'axis (C2) — permet de tourner la tête (non)
La Colonne Vertébrale et le Thorax
  • Les vertèbres cervicales (cervical) — 7
  • Les vertèbres thoraciques (thoracic) — 12
  • Les vertèbres lombaires (lumbar) — 5
  • Le sacrum — 5 vertèbres fusionnées
  • Le coccyx — 4 vertèbres fusionnées
  • La cage thoracique (thoracic cage) — 12 paires de côtes, sternum, appendice xiphoïde
  • L'appendice xiphoïde (xiphoid process) — repère pour le RCR
Les Articulations

Les 3 types d'articulations :

  • Immobiles (immovable) — sutures du crâne
  • Semi-mobiles (slightly movable) — colonne vertébrale
  • Mobiles / synoviales (freely movable) — genou, épaule, hanche

Les 5 types d'articulations mobiles :

  • Charnière (hinge) — coude, genou
  • Énarhtrose (ball-and-socket) — épaule, hanche
  • Pivot (pivot) — atlas-axis
  • En selle (saddle) — pouce
  • Plane / glissement (gliding) — poignet, cheville

Termes clés :

  • Le ménisque — amortisseur en C du genou
  • Le liquide synovial — lubrifie les articulations
  • La bourse séreuse (bursa) — sac qui réduit la friction
  • Les tendons — muscle → os (mouvement)
  • Les ligaments — os → os (stabilité)
Le Système Musculaire

Plus de 600 muscles dans le corps (40-50% du poids corporel)

Les 3 types de muscles :

  • Muscle squelettique (skeletal) — strié, volontaire
  • Muscle lisse (smooth) — non strié, involontaire
  • Muscle cardiaque (cardiac) — strié, involontaire, cœur uniquement

Muscles importants :

  • Le grand fessier (gluteus maximus) — le plus grand muscle
  • Le stapédien (stapedius) — le plus petit muscle
  • Le diaphragme — responsable de la respiration
  • Le deltoïde — site d'injection intramusculaire
  • Le tendon d'Achille — relie le mollet au talon
Termes Musculaires Clés
  • Le fascicule (fascicle) — groupe de fibres musculaires
  • Le fascia — tissu conjonctif entourant les muscles
  • Épimysium — couche externe
  • Périmysium — couche moyenne
  • Endomysium — couche interne
  • Hypertrophie (hypertrophy) — croissance musculaire par surcharge
  • Atrophie (atrophy) — fonte musculaire par inactivité
  • Contracture — muscle figé en position fléchie
Soins Préopératoires
  • NPO (nil per os) — rien par la bouche, à jeun après minuit
  • Le consentement chirurgical — signé par le patient ou son tuteur
  • Retirer : bijoux, prothèses, lentilles, vernis à ongles
  • Le vernis à ongles — interfère avec l'oxymètre de pouls
  • Vérifier : bracelet d'identité, bracelet d'allergies, groupe sanguin
  • Préparation psychologique — évaluer l'anxiété, expliquer les attentes
Soins Postopératoires
  • Les 7 P — Douleur, Pouls, Pâleur, Paresthésie, Paralysie, Pression, Poïkilothermie
  • Le spiromètre incitatif — 10 fois par heure pour prévenir l'atélectasie
  • Mobilisation précoce — prévient la TVP, la pneumonie, les escarres
  • La pompe PCA — analgésie contrôlée par le patient
  • Le Narcan (naloxone) — antagoniste des opioïdes, renverse la dépression respiratoire
  • Effets secondaires des opioïdes — dépression respiratoire, constipation, nausées
  • Uriner dans les 8 heures après la chirurgie
  • Signes d'infection — rougeur, gonflement, chaleur, écoulement
